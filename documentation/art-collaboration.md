# Art Collaboration With AI

## How I figured out how to help AI figure out what I wanted.

By Jeff Townsend

Since the commercial introduction of Generative Art by OpenAI's Dall-e,
artists have had to face a reckoning with Artificial Intelligence's role
as \"artists.\" After a brief, novelistic awe, we had to ask ourselves:
Was this what we were seeing? NFTs had very recently become a thing,
valuating digital art in a ghastly, profound manner. Collectors could
\"own\" them in a way that proved provenance allowing JPEGs (and other
digital files) (ie. zeros and ones) to have real world, monetary value
with the Art World. NFTs ultimately crashed, but the reason I point this
out, is to illustrate the tremendous push and pull digital art, and in
turn digital artists, experienced in a very short period of time. One
day they can become rich off of the non-traditional art form, then, less
than a year later, anyone could create something in that ilk, using
natural language. That's it. No other skills necessary beyond being able
to articulate what you wanted to see. *Or create?*

This text isn't a history of AI art. Nor is it an argument for or
against its ethical implications, its value, or its place in Art. AI +
Art is not going back in the bottle. Artists, and non-artists alike,
*will* use it to create \"art.\" End of story. So then, the debate must
revolve around *how* it will be used. Is there an acceptable application
of its capabilities *in* art, one of the preeminent *human*
accomplishments and endeavors? ... \"Acceptable.\" ... Yes, that is a
weighted and highly subjective term to be introduced to this profound
conundrum. But this will be the parameter from which future generations
will judge our utilization of one of, if not the greatest technological
breakthroughs the universe, as we know it, has created. It's *a lot* of
pressure. And yes, we will most certainly be judged by how we *chose* to
use this tool and what we deemed acceptable.

### It's a Tool ... for now

At the time of writing this, AI is still *merely* a tool. By that I mean
it is largely inert and takes human intervention in order to instigate
the process - to crank the gears, if you will. But I definitely believe
this is brief reprieve. However, the decisions we, as artists, make in
terms of \"acceptability\" could potentially have lasting implications.
Having said that, at this time I'm not here to offer either a judgement
or the criteria within this context. Frankly, I'm not terribly
interested in subscribing to or helping to implement any dogmatic
constitution at this juncture, especially when we haven't even begun to
scratch the surface the application capabilities are with AI + Art. I'll
defer that to others, and yourselves.

What I am interested in, though, is how *I* can use this tool within my
personal aspirations while adhering to my profound reverence for Art -
the emotional profundity, ingenuity, dedication to skill, the lineage,
how it has impacted and changed me, and so on. In the numerous
experiments I've undertaken so far, this ethos has been imprinted upon
the framework. Once again, this is personal, but I can certainly say
that I've encountered a threshold - a point where I feel that I'm
bordering on losing what I'd qualify as authorship. *And that is
important to me*. With that establishing a baseline, this is how I used
AI, as a tool, and in turn, a collaborator, for the video art work,
*Taken Pictures (2024).*

### Prologue to the Process

Before I outline and explain the AI tools I used, and *why* I used these
tools within this project, I want to reintroduce and expound on a couple
of concepts I touched upon earlier. First, is \"art as articulation.\"
In other words, the ability to use natural language, alone, to create
art. As you may have gleaned from the title and subtitle of this paper,
language and communication played an integral role in realizing my
vision - accomplishing what I set out to create. But even at the outset,
while in the midst of a huge communication breakdown, that I was
honestly unaware was even taking place, I recognized this massive
opportunity and sea change for artists like myself. Which is to say,
artists whose curiosity, influence, and aspirations greatly outweigh
their abilities to create what they've conceptualized. Think of a
filmmaker who wrote a script for a feature-length, FX heavy, space
opera. That filmmaker, though, is without the financial means to hire
the hundreds of specialists - from sound designers to set builders to
special effects artists and makeup artists, costume design, and so on -
needed to see the script become a film. That filmmaker can, of course,
learn every single one of those highly specialized skills then act every
role themself, or, just not make it.
